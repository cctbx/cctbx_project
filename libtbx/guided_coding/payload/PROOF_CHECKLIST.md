# The proof checklist

Every line resolves to exactly one of: VERIFIED, RECORDED, FAILED, NOT APPLICABLE (reason from the profile, or from the packet's outcome class as line 16 defines), or WAIVED (waiving authority quoted). A bare tick is not a result.

VERIFIED and RECORDED are different acts and are never blurred. VERIFIED is used only where the checker tested the predicate itself against the packet's own contents - a hash matched, a quoted failure reason present, a roster compared. RECORDED is used where the line rests on a judgment: the checker confirms that the judgment was explicitly made, by the required person, about identified evidence - and adopts none of it as its own. "The decisive test failed on the baseline, output quoted" is VERIFIED; "the change conforms to the coding standards document" is RECORDED. A checker that writes VERIFIED against a judgment has overstated what it knows. The checker subagent signs each line against the frozen packet identified by its hash; the sheet names that hash.

0. `PROOF_SUMMARY.md` is present as the packet's first file, follows `.claude/PROOF_SUMMARY_TEMPLATE.md`, and its six sections are each supported by evidence elsewhere in this packet. A reviewer could read it alone and know what was wrong, what was done, what was tested, what the results were, and whether the job is complete.

NOTE TO THE CHECKER: you have reading tools only and cannot compute hashes - do not mark this FAILED for that reason. Verify that the manifest exists, that it lists every file present, and that the author's own hash check is recorded and labelled as corroboration rather than independent verification. Whether a checker should ever be able to hash is a procedure question, not this packet's defect.
13. The live tree is restored and verified BY CONTENT; the tree lock is released; no scratch tst files exist under .claude. Where an inventory of protected files is cited, it must separate the DEVELOPER'S files from the procedure's own files: a procedure package installed during the change will otherwise appear as failing checksums on protected data, which is alarming and false.
14. The incident log is present (empty or not); any incident shows its five steps and the developer-authorized recovery.
15. The approval audit lists every prompt that reached the developer, with causes.
16. The packet names the OUTCOME CLASS it supports - published, saved locally, discarded, not-reproduced/stopped, no candidate (no packet is assembled; the record is the deliverable), or PUBLICATION BATCH (the lines about a decisive test and construction are satisfied by each constituent change's own record inside the packet, or marked not applicable with that reason; the lines about review are satisfied, per constituent, EITHER by the constituent's own review in its record OR by the batch's light-constituent review recorded in the batch record's Review section - every light-path constituent - test-checked or inspection-checked - must have the latter, since the light path skips review; a batch whose Review section is not applicable while any light-path constituent exists FAILS this line) - and every line above was evaluated under that class (a not-reproduced packet resolves lines 3-7 NOT APPLICABLE with that reason). Approvals are NOT part of this packet: the checker assesses evidence readiness only, and the approvals record is a separate document, outside the frozen packet, referencing its identity hash.
